Pharmacy Closure Tracker

The pharmacy closures the registries miss - recorded from state board rosters before the boards delete them.

Download: this window (CSV) · cumulative (CSV) · latest.json · RSS · data dictionary · CC BY 4.0

Why this exists. State pharmacy boards publish today's license roster and delete yesterday's. When a pharmacy closes, most of the boards recorded here publish no terminal status - the license simply stops appearing (Illinois and Massachusetts, which keep closed licenses in their files with a status, are the exceptions). In Texas, of 5,871 licenses that vanished between 2014 and 2026 (5,864 never returned), 92.7% were still marked "Active" the last time they were listed (full analysis). The federal NPI registry can keep listing them as active long after. This tracker snapshots the rosters of 16 jurisdictions (15 states + DC) daily/weekly and publishes what changed, so closures are recorded at the moment they happen instead of being lost.

Twelve years of Texas, reconstructed

Texas keeps no history of its own roster, so the only record of it before this tracker was 49 Internet Archive captures of the file (2014-2025). We replayed them: 5,871 licenses vanished, 74% of the 2014 roster; four rural counties lost their only pharmacy and never got one back; Harris County (Houston) is down 132 licenses while Fort Bend County is up exactly 132; 43.8% of every independent pharmacy ever seen has vanished versus 20.3% of chain sites. What the other boards publish

A 51-jurisdiction survey of what each state board of pharmacy exposes for pharmacy sites - downloadable file or not, status, issue date, dated closure, and whether closed licenses stay in the file: state board scorecard. Only a handful publish a dated closure; in most, removal from the roster is the only trace.

Method

  • Sources. The public site-license roster of each board, fetched as published (CSV/XLSX/GeoJSON) - Texas daily, others weekly. Payloads are stored raw and content-hashed; a day on which the board republished an identical file stores nothing.
  • Events. Keyed on the board's own license number. New = key present now, absent before. Removed = key present before, absent now (a "removed while Active" row is one whose last published status was Active/Current). Status change and name change on a persisting key.
  • Renewal re-listings are excluded. Some boards (Connecticut) publish only licenses already renewed for the coming term, so during renewal season decades-old pharmacies "appear". An appearance whose original issue date is more than 90 days before the earlier snapshot is classed as a re-listing and not counted; so is one whose issue date is blank or later than the snapshot in a source that normally publishes issue dates (a renewal-term date published as an "issue date"). Conservative rules; they can only lower the counts.
  • Chain vs independent. A name match against a list of national and regional chain and banner names (ported from the Texas analysis and extended); everything else is "independent-or-unclassified". Approximate by construction - hospital, clinic and small regional chains fall into the second bucket, and a site renamed by an acquirer takes the acquirer's class.
  • Relicensing. A removal whose street address reappears among the same window's new licenses (same city) is flagged as a probable ownership change or relocation rather than a closure.
  • How this differs from other closure data. Commercial place datasets (e.g. SafeGraph, ChainXY) infer closures from web or foot-traffic signals at month resolution and carry no license number; NCPDP's pharmacy database carries a self-reported closure date that researchers have found unreliable. This tracker is regulator-primary: the board's own license number, the board's own status, day-resolved (Texas) or week-resolved, with status and name transitions and an immutable snapshot archive behind every row.
  • Removal is not proof of closure. It is removal of the license from the public roster. In the Texas replay 99.9% of removals were never seen on the roster again (7 of 5,871 were) - but confirm individually before relying on any one row.
  • Limits. Weekly cadence brackets an event to a week (Texas: a day). Coverage is 16 jurisdictions (15 states + DC; Florida, Nevada, Mississippi and Pennsylvania from 2026-08-16); more states are added as their exports are validated. Bulk exports change format without notice; every build re-checks row counts against expected ranges and refuses to publish a state that fails.
